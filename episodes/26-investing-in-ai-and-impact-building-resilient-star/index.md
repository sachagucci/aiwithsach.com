## Summary

Sach talks with Cédric Beaudoin, who invests in Series A and later technology companies at Investissement Québec, with a focus on impact. Cédric says he respects entrepreneurs because of the sacrifice and grit the job demands. He describes impact in terms of CO2 reduction, circularity, regional effects and social benefit. For successful startups he points to a long-lasting mission and agility, using Hopper's many pivots as the example. He also argues that shipping something imperfect and fixing it later beats chasing perfection. From his time with the NextAI cohort, he says AI is not new, but the tools and compute are. Startups should build vertical products by assembling existing models into modular software that lets them swap components as better ones appear. He gives two examples: Ready Plan Go for personal tax returns, and Clear Estate for estate planning, where about 40 billion dollars is expected to change hands in ten years. The conversation then turns to how technology can ease an emotionally heavy process while keeping a human in the loop. Cédric names the startup he wishes someone would pitch, a platform linking contractors and suppliers. He closes by arguing that AI is now a feature of every company and that everyone, not only PhDs, should talk about it.

## Key takeaways

1. Impact investors weigh CO2 reduction, circularity, regional effects and social benefit, and CO2 is only the easiest of these to quantify.
2. Resilient startups combine a long-lasting mission with the agility to pivot, as Hopper did many times.
3. Ship something imperfect and fix it later: startups can iterate fast because few people are working on the same large problem.
4. Vertical AI startups can win by building modular software that plugs in existing models and lets them swap one out when a better one appears.
5. In emotionally heavy areas like estate planning, automate the forms and paperwork, and keep a human in the loop to speak the same language as the client.
6. Talk about AI in everyday terms with people around you and start with the tools you can use today, rather than AGI or white papers.
