## Summary

Sach talks with JC Btaiche, CEO and founder of Fuse, a Canadian fusion company. JC explains fusion in plain terms: reproducing the conditions inside the sun for a few nanoseconds. Fuse does this with what he calls a lightning bolt in a bottle, a pulse of stored energy released very fast to implode fuel. He traces his path from a childhood wish to build a fusion-powered rocket, helped by a father trained as a nuclear physicist, to a conviction that energy underpins human prosperity. He links GDP per capita to kilowatt hours available per capita. JC describes Fuse's milestones: the first licence in Canada for its type of fusion generator, a new pulse power machine tested over 100 shots, and early commercial revenue, which sets it apart from firms betting on energy in 7 to 10 years. He presents Titan, a pulse power driver that could make fusion facilities a third of the size, five times cheaper and twice as energy efficient. He frames the future through Vaclav Smil's energy ages, argues for tracking the rate of progress instead of a date, and notes the defence roots of the technology. He closes by inviting listeners to a live firing of Titan on August 8.

## Key takeaways

1. Fusion copies how the sun works: compress light atoms under extreme pressure, density and time until they fuse and release energy.
2. AI and quantum computing are pushing energy demand up sharply, which is why new energy sources matter to the AI story.
3. GDP per capita tracks the kilowatt hours available per capita, so energy access shapes prosperity, especially in emerging economies.
4. Fuse's approach, pulse power derived from decades of national lab research, aims to make fusion facilities smaller, cheaper and more efficient.
5. Some fusion companies bet on commercial energy in 7 to 10 years. Fuse sells technology and earns revenue today while maturing the science.
6. JC suggests judging fusion by its rate of progress and iteration rather than by a promised date.
