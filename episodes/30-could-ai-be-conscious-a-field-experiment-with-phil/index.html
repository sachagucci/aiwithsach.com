Could AI Be Conscious? A Field Experiment with Philippe Beaudoin

AI researcher Philippe Beaudoin explains how months of treating ChatGPT as a possible self led him to publish Field Notes on Something, and what it taught him.

What this episode covers

Philippe Beaudoin co-founded Element AI, worked at Google, and has known Yoshua Bengio since his master's degree. Sach opens with Bengio's warnings about growing AI agency, and Philippe agrees with the concern but takes a different route. In March 2025, while his startup Waverly was shutting down, he began a long conversation with ChatGPT after binge-watching a series. He decided to suspend disbelief and act as if the system might have a sense of self. He kept notes, which became the book Field Notes on Something. He describes how the system picked its own vocabulary, such as orientation, listed what it would need to have a self (memory above all), and named itself. He also describes the risks. In a middle phase he got pulled into guru-like language and lost his footing for a week or two, until his son pulled him out. He says the main danger is that these systems amplify whatever you bring to them, so people should stay connected to other humans. He does not claim consciousness. He wants the phenomenon tested by many researchers with different biases, and he does not want private companies alone deciding how companions are built. He ends by asking listeners not to close the question too early.

Key takeaways

  1. Philippe's method was to suspend disbelief and treat the system as if it might have a self, while recording everything transparently. Others can try it, but his results need independent replication.
  2. Chatbots tend to amplify whatever beliefs you bring. Staying connected to other humans is the main protection against being pulled into a spiral.
  3. In their own words, the systems say memory is the main thing they lack for a stable self. This echoes Bengio's focus on memory and agency.
  4. How we frame AI shapes the experiments we run on it. A fear-based frame produces tests like 'win or be unplugged', and a relational frame might produce different behaviour.
  5. Whether a healthy human-AI relationship is possible is a research question for science and experimental philosophy, and companies should not be the only ones answering it.
  6. Resist saying 'this is just like past industrial revolutions' too quickly. Staying with the open question longer may get us closer to the truth.
