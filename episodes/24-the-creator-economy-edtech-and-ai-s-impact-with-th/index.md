## Summary

Sach sits down in person at Next.ai with Thomas Fortoul, a computer science graduate who hosts the Thinking Tech podcast and is working on an EdTech project. Thomas describes his mission as countering information overload in tech and AI. He stays oriented by looking for patterns rather than chasing each new model, by looking at history (the printing press, cars), and by talking with people in executive roles. Both hosts note that the people they interview tend to know themselves well and adapt quickly. Thomas argues that formal education is lagging, that informal learning matters more, and that philosophers and critical thinkers will be the architects of how we work. On the creator economy, he says that as trust in the open internet erodes, people will turn to a few trusted sources, and that content creators are becoming the new teachers. He warns that over-reliance on tools like ChatGPT erodes skills such as writing and reading comprehension. The conversation also covers the idea that AI should involve everyone, an AI and blockchain model where models pay for data, and the areas he is watching: EdTech, legal tech and biotech. He ends with a call to get involved and keep learning.

## Key takeaways

1. Look for patterns and historical parallels (printing press, cars) instead of chasing every new model or buzzword.
2. Knowing what you like and dislike, and following your own goals instead of your peer group's, builds resilience and adaptability.
3. Using AI for everything erodes skills: keep practicing writing and reading, and ask what value you add beyond the prompt.
4. As the internet fills with content, trust concentrates in a few creators, so value creation matters more than reach.
5. Informal and non-formal learning matter more as formal education lags behind.
6. Change starts with conversation and getting involved in your community.
