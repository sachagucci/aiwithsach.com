## Summary

Sach talks with Timothy, co-founder of the AI Launch Lab, a Canadian non-profit that trains people for AI work through hands-on projects and internships. Timothy explains how it grew out of a Concordia student association in 2018, survived the pandemic funding cuts through Quebec government grants, and now focuses on college students with partners Dawson College and Mitacs. He describes why project-based learning keeps pace with AI tools that change monthly, why 75% of their cohorts come from underrepresented groups, and how companies still tend to hire only PhDs or young people. They then discuss responsible AI as a business advantage, and Timothy's reluctance to spend time on AGI speculation compared with present harms. He raises the Apple Card credit disputes, opaque lending algorithms, and the entry-level jobs disappearing first. Using the Luddites as an example, he argues that people are now artisans who learn to use the machines, and that ordinary social and political pressure will be needed to avoid a dystopia. He ends by urging people outside AI, especially those with domain knowledge, to get involved.

## Key takeaways

1. Project-based training keeps up with AI better than textbooks, because the tools and models change monthly or even weekly.
2. LLMs have cut routine data cleaning in student hackathons from about a day and a half to a few hours, so college students can now deliver real value to companies.
3. Many companies still hire only PhDs for AI work, yet many AI projects do not need that profile; diversity of background changes what gets built.
4. Ethical and responsible AI can be a business advantage: it lowers litigation risk and prepares companies for coming regulation.
5. Opaque algorithms in lending and hiring, like the Apple Card disputes, show why current harms deserve more attention than AGI speculation.
6. Entry-level jobs are the first to be stripped away, so building skills early and bringing domain expertise into AI matters.
