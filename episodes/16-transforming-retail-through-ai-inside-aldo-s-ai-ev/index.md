## Summary

Sach talks with Fatih Nayebi, Vice President of Data and AI at Aldo Group and a lecturer at McGill, about turning a traditional retailer into an AI-powered organization. Fatih describes how his office is organized: data governance, a data platform team, data product teams and a science team, with product managers from each business unit defining the problems. He walks through the revenue growth management program: demand forecasting over a 26-week season, markdown optimization and order fulfillment optimization, built with Evada Labs and partly funded by a Scale AI grant. On generative AI, he covers product and SEO descriptions, an internal RAG assistant for store employees, and a conversational, trend-aware search proof of concept from a hackathon at McGill. The conversation also covers the data problems that slow retail AI, privacy rules such as Law 25 and GDPR, a lakehouse architecture on AWS, how ROI is measured at four levels, and how projects are prioritized. Fatih closes with advice for newcomers: respect data quality, expect experiments to fail, and have people who can explain honestly what AI can and cannot do.

## Key takeaways

1. Sort each problem first: does it need intelligence (a brain problem) or just an application or process fix (a muscle problem)?
2. AI projects stall on data: models need many years of clean, complete, consistent data, not just data good enough for daily decisions.
3. Measure ROI at four levels: bottom-line impact, operational metrics, model metrics, and adoption and usage of the product.
4. Not every experiment reaches production; sometimes the data is missing or expectations are far from results, and teams should plan for that.
5. Agile development alone doesn't cover data practices; data quality, observability and governance have to be built in deliberately.
6. Keep people who can bridge technical and business teams and explain plainly what AI can and cannot do.
