AI Transformation: Democratizing Innovation at TELUS

TELUS AI Accelerator founder Alexandre Guilbault explains how to scale AI adoption: solve business problems first, fail fast, hire for soft skills, prepare for agentic AI.

What this episode covers

Alexandre Guilbault founded the TELUS AI Accelerator, a team of more than 65 AI engineers working across mobility, home solutions, health and agriculture. He explains that TELUS now wants to put generative AI in the hands of as many employees as possible, because 100,000 people thinking about uses will beat a small expert team. He separates classic machine learning, such as fraud detection, churn prediction and network capacity forecasting, from generative AI co-pilots for IT support, field technicians and call centres. His main lessons are practical. Stakeholder alignment matters more than the model. Teams should define the business problem before the solution and run proofs of value rather than proofs of concept. Classic ML projects are treated as R&D: test the weakest link first, fail within days, and rank projects by risk-weighted EBITDA. TELUS found that bundling AI with change management created resistance, so each business unit now names a change champion. Squads mix the same roles and are tied to one business unit. Hiring favours curiosity, humility and resourcefulness over credentials. Alexandre advises newcomers to centralize AI talent and to accept that most projects fail. He sees agentic AI, where AI acts as a peer and not only a counselor, as the next wave, with open governance questions. He also argues that management practices must evolve alongside the technology.

Key takeaways

  1. Technology, people and process must change together; the best model is useless if nobody changes how they work.
  2. Start from the business problem, not the AI solution, and prefer a proof of value over a proof of concept.
  3. Treat classic ML as R&D: test the riskiest assumption first, fail within days, and rank projects by risk-weighted value.
  4. Name a change champion inside each business unit so ownership of process change is clear.
  5. Hire for curiosity, humility and resourcefulness; in a field that moves this fast, learning speed matters more than credentials.
  6. Fund AI as a portfolio, not project by project: if about 80% of data science projects fail, only a portfolio view keeps funding alive.
