## Summary

Sach talks with Benedikt, co-founder of Jamie, a Berlin-based AI meeting assistant. Benedikt tells how he and his co-founder Luis met playing tennis, started a first product called TeamTap, and then built Jamie to solve a problem they felt themselves: taking meeting notes while trying to listen. Jamie listens to a call or an in-person meeting without a bot joining, then produces action items, decisions, a customizable summary and a transcript. A sidebar lets users ask questions across their past meetings. Benedikt explains that they launched an imperfect first version in 2022, personally onboarded a couple of hundred customers, and grew through SEO and close contact with users. On regulation, he says Jamie is GDPR compliant, stores no audio permanently, does not train on user data and hosts data in Germany. He sees AI as a productivity driver if companies invest in education, because many people are still afraid of it. His advice to founders: do not treat polite interest as validation. Launch a simple MVP, or get contracts signed before you build.

## Key takeaways

1. Solving a problem you have yourself brings more passion than solving one for strangers.
2. Launching an imperfect first version early gives the feedback needed to build a solid core product.
3. Personally onboarding early users and staying in touch with them is a growth channel as well as a learning one.
4. Polite interest is not validation: ship a simple MVP, or get people to sign contracts or pay before you build.
5. For an AI tool used at work, privacy choices (GDPR, no training on user data, EU hosting) can be a selling point.
6. Companies adopting AI need education and clear guidelines, because the first reaction of many employees is fear.
