How Salesforce is Using AI to Transform Customer Relationships

A Salesforce solutions engineer explains how AI is added to CRM, why trust and humans in the loop matter, and why he expects a return to human connection.

What this episode covers

Sach talks with Jag, a senior solutions engineer at Salesforce, about how AI is being built into customer relationship management. Jag starts with his background in data analytics and his view that the more useful question is what remains once the AI hype settles. He compares it to Napster, which made music abundant and less valued, followed by a rise in vinyl sales. He describes Salesforce tools such as Prompt Builder, Copilot and a vector database. He gives an example from a mining company that cut its response time for bid documents (RFIs) from 175 days to under 30 using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). He explains the trust layer between the language model and customer data, the audit trail, and why a human stays in the loop for B2B uses. He sees RAG as an underdog technology and the AI boom as a comeback for plain machine learning. He expects healthcare and law to be disrupted. He says he would like an AI spam filter so real emails stand out, and admits he sometimes jumps to ChatGPT before listening to a customer's question. He ends by asking listeners to think about what they want automated in two years, and to accept being imperfect.

Key takeaways

  1. Ask what remains of an AI use case once the hype fades, not only what is exciting now.
  2. Salesforce puts a trust layer between the language model and customer data: no training on customer data, an audit trail of queries and toxicity detection.
  3. RAG over past documents can cut very long, repetitive work, such as a bid response going from 175 days to under 30.
  4. In serious B2B settings, AI replaces tasks, not people, and a human stays in the loop.
  5. Much of the current AI boom is classic machine learning (regression, likelihood to close) under a new name.
  6. Before reaching for ChatGPT, listen to the question first; being empathetic is a skill AI can erode.
