## Summary

Amogh Chaturvedi, co-founder of Human Behavior, explains how his startup uses vision models to watch session replays, the recordings of customers using a product, and say why users stay, leave or pay. The idea came out of a boba shop brainstorm after YC partners rejected two earlier pitches, an AI accounting tool and a neobank for e-commerce. Amogh admits those were ideas his team picked without any personal connection to the problem, while session replay was a pain they had felt themselves. He describes the hardest technical part, which is scale: some customers send a million sessions a day, and Gemini hallucinates. He also describes how the team validated before building, by begging for calls with product teams, talking to over a hundred people and tallying bullet points on a whiteboard after each call. The conversation also covers YC's ten-minute interview, why the community matters more than the advice, lean experiments, and co-founder luck. Amogh is open about his limits: he is a first-time founder, and says so repeatedly. His advice is to find a hair-on-fire problem and not build until buyers say yes.

## Key takeaways

1. Pick ideas you have personally lived: the team's two rejected YC ideas had no personal link, while session replay came from a pain they already had.
2. Validate before building: Human Behavior talked to over a hundred product people and tallied the repeated points on a whiteboard to decide what to work on.
3. If the problem is urgent enough, buyers forgive bugs and a rough onboarding.
4. AI analysis is limited by the data it gets: a single landing page gives too little signal to analyze.
5. Scale is the real engineering test for AI video analysis: millions of sessions a day, model hallucinations and a need for solid infrastructure.
6. Early on, the YC network can matter more than the advice: another batch company helped fix their infrastructure for free.
