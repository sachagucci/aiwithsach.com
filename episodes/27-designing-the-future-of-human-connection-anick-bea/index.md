## Summary

Sach talks with Anick Beaulieu, CEO of C2, an experiential studio that designs B2B events for brands like EY, Amazon and Meta and runs the C2 Montréal flagship. Anick says AI changes events and also doesn't: the goal is still to design meaningful human connections, and AI is a tool to do that better. She is skeptical of the pressure to adopt AI, noting that marketing makes everyone feel behind, and asks what a team is trying to achieve before asking whether to use AI. She sees AI speeding up the path from concept to prototype, but says it will shrink some teams, and that clients hesitate to buy fully AI-made creative work because ownership and IP are unclear. On data, she wants AI to measure emotion at events and trace their long-term effects, such as partnerships that start years later. As a leader, she says AI hasn't changed her style but adds urgency, and that she gives space to staff who know the tools better than she does. She tells a Nike ad story to explain why resisting change doesn't work. The episode ends with a quick-fire round and a plug for C2 Montréal.

## Key takeaways

1. Ask what you are trying to achieve before asking whether to use AI; the goal should drive the tool.
2. AI shortens the path from idea to testable concept, but the human still has to come up with the concept and own it.
3. Unclear IP ownership is a practical limit today: clients hesitate to buy fully AI-made creative work.
4. Leaders who are not tech experts can give space to team members who are, and let their roles grow beyond the original job description.
5. Resisting change outright blocks useful discussion; the productive question is how to adopt AI, not whether.
6. The hardest thing to measure about events is long-term impact, and emotion tracking is one area where AI could help.
