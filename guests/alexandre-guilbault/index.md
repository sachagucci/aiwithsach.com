# Alexandre Guilbault

Alexandre Guilbault (Founder of the TELUS AI Accelerator, leading a team of over 65 AI engineers at TELUS) was a guest on AI with Sach, a podcast hosted by Sacha Gucciardo in Montréal.

## Episode 19: AI Transformation: Democratizing Innovation at TELUS (December 2024)

TELUS AI Accelerator founder Alexandre Guilbault explains how to scale AI adoption: solve business problems first, fail fast, hire for soft skills, prepare for agentic AI.

Page: https://aiwithsach.com/episodes/19-ai-transformation-democratizing-innovation-at-telu/

> I'm often saying the best model in the world is useless if nobody use it or nobody change their process out of that. So sometimes I feel with new technologies coming in, people are so impressed by the technology itself that they forget the principle that has always been there and is still there, that there's no meaningful change without technology, people and process.

> POCs are really popular in technology, but a proof of concept finishes with technology concepts. It's not necessarily creating value. So we prefer proof of value. And a proof of value, you might not even have the technology concept completely finalized.
> — at 7:45 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YvitzP-4VA0&t=465s)

> If you don't learn every single day in six months, you're a dinosaur. So even if you hire the best person in the world, but he doesn't have the curiosity, resourcefulness, humility to continue to learn and question others around and read, they will be out of the game within six months and a year.
> — at 20:38 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YvitzP-4VA0&t=1238s)

> But some studies have proven that 80% of the data science project in companies fails to deliver value. So that's four projects out of five that will fail. Finance thinks about funding one project and expects a return on one project. They are more likely not to see the return, and they will stop funding AI.
> — at 25:39 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YvitzP-4VA0&t=1539s)

> We really think that having 100,000 people thinking about the best way to use those technologies is much better than having a small team of experts in the domain trying to figure out how those technologies can really transform business students.
